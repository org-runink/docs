Body, activity and weather

Body, activity and weather

Everything Luna knows about your body comes through the profile page (⋯ → Profile & connections) and a handful of ProfileService records. It is all health data. Only the trainer and nutritionist aspects may have it put into their prompts (see The companion).

Your profile

What Luna knows holds the facts coaching is built on:

Field Notes
Body mass, goal body mass, height Every meal target is anchored to body mass. "No goal" is a valid state, and Luna never invents one from your direction.
Direction lose, maintain or gain
Units metric or imperial, display only. Everything is kept in metric.
Birth year, sex Optional. Sex is never inferred or defaulted.
Dietary preferences, equipment, constraints Fed to the plan generators
Profession, current focus, routine environment Context for the model only ("train in the evening" is wrong advice if your evenings are taken). None of these change a calorie target.

Updates merge: only the fields you send are written, so telling Luna "I'm 82kg now" doesn't erase your height. The server rejects implausible values with messages like "N cm is not a plausible height".

Data connections

Connection Source Platform
Steps Health Connect (Android), Google Fit (web test build) Android and the web test build. See Habits and streaks.
Body composition Health Connect Android
Sleep and heart rate Health Connect ("Last night from your ring") Android
Local weather Open-Meteo, fetched by the app, opt-in Android and the web test build (iOS once built). See The meadow backdrop.

Luna only reads from Health Connect. It requests no write permission.

Body composition

Each scale reading (BodyComposition) records when it was taken, weight, body-fat %, BMI, fat-free mass, and whatever else the scale reports. It also records a source (manual, scale_photo or health_connect) and whether it was read off a screen by a vision model (photo_derived). Zero means "not reported" on every field, so the app shows a missing reading as missing rather than as 0%. A measured BMR is used in your meal plan, and the plan says so.

Last night

A wearable day (WearableDay) groups sleep, activity and stress reports for your local day, so a sleep that starts at 23:10 belongs to the night you'd call it. The short "insights" text is never generated by a model. Any note the device itself supplied is kept verbatim, credited to the device, and never presented as Luna's words.

The wearable's step count is used as context only. It is never counted toward your streak, so the same walk is never counted twice.

Tracked activities

⋯ → Track an activity records a GPS walk, run or ride. It needs location permission ("Luna needs location permission to track a route."). When you save, two things are written:

  1. A workout habit event, so the day counts toward the training streak.
  2. An activity summary (RecordActivity) with the kind, start time, distance, and both moving time and elapsed time. The app says which one a pace uses.

The route never leaves your phone. Only the summary is stored. The map under the live track is drawn from OpenStreetMap tiles, which means OpenStreetMap sees the tile requests. Past activities are listed under Saved activities.

Your own account of how you run

Inner context is three lists you write, stored verbatim:

  • Baseline: how you work when things are normal.
  • Stressors: what is costing you right now, unranked.
  • Strategies: what you already know helps.

It exists so coaching stops ignoring the person receiving it. For example, it keeps a hard evening session out of a week you have flagged as bad.

  • Luna never writes, infers or edits it.
  • It is not a diagnosis, score or trend. There is no severity and no history.
  • It changes what Luna suggests, not what Luna talks about. The prompt tells Luna never to quote it back or raise it unprompted.
  • Only counsel, trainer and nutritionist may read it. It has its own scope, separate from the health journal.
  • To clear it, save it empty (UpdateInnerContext with an empty message). Each update replaces the whole record, and from then on nothing reads the old text. Earlier versions may remain until they are deleted on request (see Privacy and data).
