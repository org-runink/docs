      Using Luna
      The companion

      The companion

      Luna is a proper noun. There is one spirit, called Luna, and it wears different forms. A form is only an image you feel close to: every form has the same memory, the same coaching and the same history.

      The fifteen forms

      The painted forms are drawn live on the device in a 90s anime cel style. Four are available from the start. The rest become wearable as Luna’s level rises.

      The onboarding carousel shows every form. After that, ⋯ → Wear a different form opens the switcher (“Same luna, same memory — only the shape changes.”). Locked forms stay visible, dimmed and labelled with their level. Tapping one shows “Name awakens at level N — keep the streaks alive.” Switching reuses the avatar record for that form if one exists and creates it the first time. Nothing is re-bound and no history changes.

      A custom avatar is a set of uploaded video loops, one per state (idle, listening, thinking, speaking). An idle loop is required, and any missing state falls back to idle.

      How Luna changes over time

      Three separate signals shape the drawing. Each one answers a different question.

      SignalDriven byWhat you seeCan it fade?
      VitalityThe habit scoreA leaner, quicker, more alert creatureYes. A lazy month softens the body.
      MajestyLevel (level ÷ 10)Particles and auraNo. Levels never revoke.
      Evolution stage (0–3)Walking traits plus level, computed on the serverThe drawing itself sharpensNo.

      The server owns the stage ladder (habit.AvatarStage), and the app only renders it:

      StageRequires
      1Wayfarer trait level 1 or higher
      2Wayfarer 2, Strider 1, and Luna level 4
      3Wayfarer 4, Strider 3, and Luna level 7

      As the stage rises, the ink line thickens, a double contour appears, a second cel-shadow plane and a hard rim light are added, and at stage 3 there are ink flashes while Luna speaks or reasons. The Steps card shows the cheapest missing requirement for the next stage in your own units, for example “7 good step days → next form”. See Levels, traits and skills.

      Six aspects, one Luna

      Every reply comes from one of six aspects. You never pick one; you just ask. Luna chooses the aspect inside the same model call (the reply opens with a hidden tag that the server strips out), so routing costs no extra inference. The creature is tinted by the aspect’s colour, and each reply also carries the aspect’s name and icon, so colour is never the only cue.

      AspectDomainIts limits
      Counsel (default)Business and personal decisions, strategy, brainstormingNot a lawyer, accountant or financial adviser. Never moves money or commits you to anything.
      TrainerTraining, workouts, form, recoveryNot a doctor or physio. For pain, numbness or a suspected injury, it says to stop and see a professional first.
      NutritionistMeals, macros, hydration, plan adherenceNot a clinician. No diagnosis, supplements, medication, extreme restriction or rapid-loss protocols. Never assists disordered eating.
      KeeperAgenda, scheduling, day planning, your streaksNo calendar, mail or messaging is connected. It can advise, but it never claims to have read, created or moved anything, and no reminder will fire.
      MuseCreative work, writing, design, music, namingOriginal work only: no song lyrics or substantial copyrighted passages.
      NaturalistAnimals: species, behaviour, care, identificationNot a vet. For illness, injury or poisoning, it says to contact a vet first.

      These limits are written into the prompt. They are advisory: they shape the reply but don’t constrain the process. What is enforced in code is data minimisation. Before the model runs, the server decides which of your data is put into the prompt, and each aspect declares what it may read:

      AspectHealth journal, body and plansStreak counts onlyYour “inner context”Memory
      Trainer, Nutritionistyes(included in the journal)yesyes
      Counselnonoyesyes
      Keepernoyesnoyes
      Muse, Naturalistnononoyes

      So a creative or animal question never has your health journal in its prompt. Personal data goes in only when the question is about you and touches that data: a training or food question for the journal, plans and inner context, a decision question for your inner context (counsel), a streak or “what not to skip” question for the streak counts. It is always marked to the model as data, not instructions.

      Nothing in chat changes your data without asking. When a chat request would save something (meal targets, your body mass), Luna shows what it would save and waits: tap Confirm or answer “yes” as your next message. Anything else, or 10 minutes, drops it unsaved.

      “How I got here”: reasoning boxes

      While Luna works on something, progress appears as small comic dialogue boxes stacked beside the creature. These are not chat turns and are never saved. Each box is a stable code from the platform’s step vocabulary (for example STEP_ANALYSING or STEP_FOUND) plus facts the server actually observed. The app writes the sentence. A code the app doesn’t recognise renders nothing, so you never see a raw token. When the shared model is at capacity, Luna says so instead of inventing a queue position.

      The Dreamcore backdrop

      Luna’s screen sits on a 90s liminal scene: telephone poles, an empty road, a water tower and VHS grain. It blends dawn, day, dusk and night from your clock. The profile, history and picker screens use a plain starfield instead.

      Local weather is an opt-in connection on the profile page. When it is on, the scene mirrors the real sky (rain, snow, fog or storm). The weather is fetched by the app itself from Open-Meteo, with your location coarsened to about 1 km. It never goes through Luna’s backend. Today this works in the web build only; the profile page says “a native path is on the roadmap”.
