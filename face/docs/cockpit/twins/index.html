      Twins

      Twins

      Twins is where FACE’s decisions are acted on. The digital twins propose actions, and nothing goes out to a third party until a person approves it. The TWINS section of the rail has two entries:

      • Map, the Twins landing page: your assets where they are, with their actionable cards on the map.
      • Today’s command: the run’s actions as one list by category, each with its pipeline.

      The twin entities are on the Map too, in a panel over it.

      Old links to the twins page open Today’s command.

      Map

      The map and Today’s command show the selected run: what that one fetch found and the actions it drafted. With no run selected they show Your operation’s story starts with a fetch, with Run a fetch and Open an earlier run.

      Map is a cozy, low-poly 3-D map. It draws your assets as extruded blocks at the physical location your data sources state, with the twins’ decisions on them. Open it with Map under TWINS in the rail (/twins/map).

      What the map draws

      • Placed assets: an asset is placed only where its source stated a position, such as a GPS fix. FACE geocodes nothing by guesswork. Vehicles come from a connection you mark as telemetry. On its card under Integrations › Data sources, add {"runink.domain": "telemetry"} to Properties (JSON object). Its rows then place each vehicle they name (asset_id, or else vehicle_id or trailer_id), at the latest latitude/longitude the row itself carries, at the row’s own time. Mark the connection that holds your stops {"runink.domain": "shipments"}: each stop rides the vehicle its row names. A stop with only a city or an address stays under Not on the map, because FACE geocodes nothing.
      • Route proposals: a route is drawn in its proposed stop order. The line joins this card’s stated positions in the proposed order. It is not a road. Tap a route to see its stops, the basis of its distance and its decision.
      • Live connection layers: one layer per active connection that reports positions. A connection switched off on Fetch has no layer.
      • Site plans: the footprints of your site plans, from BIM/CAD plan files (IFC or DXF), for physical-storage awareness. Tap one to open it in the twin details.
      • Entities: the twin entities discovered in your data, such as the warehouses, yards and docks a source states with coordinates. Each placed entity is a pin with its name, so a warehouse stays findable at a regional zoom. See Twin entities.

      The map shows only located things. The twin categories that filter the queue are on Today’s command, not on the map.

      The banner at the top tells you what is on the map from your data, for example On the map from your data: 3 sites, 0 vehicles, 0 routes. When no vehicle positions have arrived yet, it says so. With nothing placed at all, it says what a fetch will place and what the fetch is doing now.

      Select a place

      Tap a warehouse, a vehicle, a route or a live feature, and its card opens on the map, beside it. A link from Today’s command (/twins/map?asset=<id> or /twins/map?route=<id>) opens that vehicle’s or route’s card the same way. Only one card is open at a time. Esc, the ✕ button or a tap on the map closes it. With a keyboard, open On the map at the bottom left and choose an entry. The card opens beside that place, and stays with it while you pan.

      A site plan’s footprint opens that plan in the twin details side panel, and every place’s card has Site plans, which opens the plans bound to it there.

      Every card opens with one line saying what happened, why it matters and what happens next, for example Demand at North DC spiked on 2026-10-01: 120 units against about 11 a day. Everything on a card comes from the fetch run selected at the top of the page. A figure that run does not contain says not measured, and says why.

      • A warehouse shows its outbound demand and any spike days, its inventory safety nets, its cold chain, its policies, and its shippers and suppliers. See A warehouse’s panel.
      • A vehicle shows its predictive maintenance: the component, the predicted failure, the signals behind it and the next action the twin drafted. The predicted failure window is not measured, because no maintenance finding carries one. Its cold chain shows any excursion in the run. The vehicle’s pending cards follow, each with its own Approve and Reject.
      • A route shows its mode, its temperature band, its re-plan (kilometres in the current order against the proposed order) and what triggered it. A mode or a band appears only when your stop rows state one (transport_mode, temperature_min_c, temperature_max_c). Otherwise the card says it is not stated. The proposal and its decision follow.

      On the map itself:

      • A route whose load has a temperature band, or whose vehicle has a cold-chain reading in the run, is drawn in ice blue instead of cyan.
      • A leg your stops state as rail, sea or air is dashed: short dashes for rail, long dashes for sea, dots for air. Road, or no stated mode, is a solid line.
      • The faded dashed line beside a route is the order it would replace.
      • A warehouse whose demand spiked in the selected run has a red ring.

      Not on the map lists the vehicles and sites your data names but the map cannot place, each with its reason: No location in the data, or Address only — waiting for the FACE geocoder. FACE never geocodes an address through a third party. Cards that are not about a located asset are not listed here; they are on Today’s command.

      The layer panel

      Layers lists FACE’s own layers, then one layer per active connection. Each has a switch, how many features it has on the map ({count} on the map), the attribution its source requires, and how old its data is (Old data · newest {time}). A layer that cannot draw says why. Live layers could not be read: {reason} means the request failed.

      Basemap

      The map is keyless by default: FACE calls no third-party tile service. Without a basemap, it draws its own pastel ground, and every position is the one the data states (No basemap installed. FACE draws its own ground; every position is the one the data states.). If your deployment configures its own basemap, it is drawn under the map.

      The fetched data has no assets yet. and No data source is connected on this instance, so there is nothing to place. say why the map is empty. The map could not be loaded: {reason} means the request failed.

      Twin entities

      Twin entities lists the twin entities FACE discovered from any of your data sources: sites, docks, assets, devices and the rest of your digital twin, each with where it came from (for example Declared by source). It opens as a side panel over the map from Twin entities in the Layers panel, beside the Entities layer that plots the located ones (/twins/map?panel=entities). It was a section of the Reconcile page until Reconcile became Knowledge; old links to it, including /twin_entities, open the map with the panel showing.

      • All classes filters the list by entity class.
      • Search id or values searches the entities.
      • Load more reads the next page.
      • New entity adds one by hand.

      Select an entity to open its details. An empty twin says so: The twin has no entities yet. Nothing is filled in for you. Could not read the twin means the request failed; select Retry. Close the panel, or tap the map beside it, to return to the map.

      A warehouse’s panel

      Select a warehouse, yard or depot and its card opens beside it on the map. Under its story line it says which record it is and which source it comes from, for example This is WH-01 in Warehouse DB. Every figure is computed on the server from the rows the selected fetch run landed for that site, and shows its Basis. A run that landed no rows for the site shows each section as having no data. A scheduled fetch that ran before 2026-10-18 filed its rows under the schedule rather than the run, so its sections show no data:

      • Policies: the business rules FACE reconciled from tables of the same source, with their status (Aligned, Drift, Shadow or Missing).
      • Outbound demand: units a day from the site’s orders and their lines, with the spread. With at least 14 days of orders, a 7-day forecast with a 95% band follows. With fewer, the panel says how many days landed. A spike is a day more than three residual deviations above the fitted demand; it is listed, and rings the warehouse on the map.
      • Inventory safety nets: per SKU, units available and on hand from the latest inventory row, and days of cover at the current demand. Safety stock and a reorder point appear only when your data states a replenishment lead time. Otherwise the panel says that none is stated.
      • Cold chain: storage temperature and reefer capacity are not measured. No connected source states either for a site yet.
      • Verified shippers: the carriers that shipped this site’s orders, with their on-time rate and whether a rate contract is on file, each citing its tables. Without planned and delivered times, reliability is not measured.
      • Suppliers: only the ones a connected source names for the site.

      No model proposes a supplier or shipper here, and the panel says so. A section with no data says why instead of showing zero. Open the entity page opens the site’s own page, and Site plans opens the site’s twin details with its bound site plan.

      Twin details

      Each twin’s details open in the side panel over the map: select Site plans on a place’s card, tap a site plan’s footprint, select Site plans in the Layers panel, or follow a link such as /twins/map?entity=<id> or /twins/map?plan=<id>&element=<id> (/twins/site-plan and older Simulate links to a plan land here too).

      The panel tells the twin’s part of the run’s story first:

      • What we saw: what the twin is, and where your data places it (or that the data states no position).
      • Why it matters: whether a site plan is bound to it.
      • What happens next: after each analysis the twins act on this site’s work by themselves, and you approve each action in Today’s command.

      Open the entity page opens the entity’s own page.

      Site plans below it shows what FACE inferred from your BIM/CAD plan files: each plan’s levels, the elements on them (zones, docks, yard slots), and the twin entity each element is bound to. For an entity, it opens the plan bound to that twin; with none bound, it reads No site plan is bound to this twin yet — upload one below.

      • Plans come from BIM/CAD plan files (IFC or DXF). PDF and image plans are accepted too. A DWG file is refused: export it as DXF or IFC.
      • Anything the vision model finds on a PDF or a picture is marked as proposed by the model, and stays out of analysis until an operator confirms it.
      • Select an element to see its twin entity (This element is the twin {class} {id}.), or This element is not in the twin. Only a confirmed zone, dock or yard slot is written. Open in the twin opens the entity.

      Site plans were a section of Simulate until 2026-10-18, beside an agent orchestrator card that has since been removed: the twins act on their own after each analysis.

      Today’s command

      Today’s command is one list: every action the twins proposed for the selected run, grouped by category. Each action shows what the twin decided and why, where it is in its pipeline, and what you can do next. Open it with Today’s command under TWINS in the rail (/today_command).

      Filter by category

      The chips under ACTIONABLE TWIN STATUS are All, REGULATORY ASSURANCE, WORKING CAPITAL, ORDER-TO-CASH, INTEGRATED BUSINESS PLANNING, COST-TO-SERVE, CARBON-AWARE LOGISTICS, SOURCE-TO-PAY and Market & diligence. Each chip shows how many of the run’s actions it holds, for example ORDER-TO-CASH · 2. Tap a chip to show only that category; a selected chip shows a check mark as well as its colour. Recovery and indicators opens its own page.

      While a filter is on, a notice titled Filtered to {filter} says so. Show all removes the filter.

      REGULATORY ASSURANCE, WORKING CAPITAL and ORDER-TO-CASH each have a chevron button that opens that twin’s remediation screen.

      Refresh reads the run’s actions again. While a fetch is running, the page shows Updating with the latest results… and reads them again by itself until the fetch ends. Scenarios for this run opens the run’s what-if scenarios on Simulate.

      Read an action

      Each category is a heading with its count, for example 2 actions · 1 waiting for you. Inside it, the actions waiting for you come first: those whose documents state a deadline, soonest first (Dated — soonest first), then Date not established, then No deadline applies, then by priority. Actions already decided follow, marked DECIDED.

      An action shows:

      • its title and the twin’s reason, the priority chip and which stage and run produced it (FETCH, RECONCILE or SIMULATE);
      • its items, as on an order: route stops, missing documents, recommended vendors. An amount is printed only when the action states one, without a currency the record does not name;
      • Next:, the stage the action is waiting on, for example Next: Approval — waiting for your decision;
      • its pipeline, one step per stage, each marked done, now, stopped, not yet or not recorded;
      • Open the evidence, which opens the decision artifact;
      • Show on the map, on an action about a vehicle, a route or an asset. It opens the map with that vehicle’s card open (/twins/map?asset=<id>);
      • Why, which opens the reason: what the twin stated, the findings and cited records of its document, the drivers of the simulation that proposed it, and What drives what, What if… and How likely is… across the run’s simulations. With no simulation evidence it says so and offers Open Scenarios.

      The pipeline stages

      Every stage is drawn from what FACE records. A stage FACE keeps no record of says not recorded, rather than showing a step that may or may not have happened.

      ActionStages
      Claim or dispute (OS&D, FNOL, notice of intent, statement of position, subrogation demand, rate and SLA audits)Detected → Evidence gathered (citations; sealed after approval) → Dispute drafted → Approval → Sent (to whom, by email) → Response → Resolved
      Sourcing or fulfilment (RFP, spend, fulfilment, S&OP cards)Shortfall found → Order drafted → Approval → Placed → Confirmed
      Route re-planTrigger → Re-plan computed (km saved) → Approval → Mappings restructured → Applied
      Predictive maintenanceSignal detected → Intervention drafted → Approval → Work scheduled
      Remediation orderFinding → Procedure derived → Assigned → Approval → Sent (the channel a person marked) → Response
      Any other decisionDrafted → Approval → Sent or Carried out

      What is not recorded, today:

      • Response and Resolved: FACE reads no reply to what it sends and keeps no closed or paid state for a claim.
      • Dispute drafted, on a derived claim document: no dispute text is written for it. An action the twin agent wrote carries its own email draft.
      • Placed and Confirmed: FACE places no ERP order. An approval records not placed: erp:not_implemented.
      • Mappings restructured and Applied: approving a route records the decision; it sends nothing to a vehicle or a TMS.
      • Sent, on an approved action, is done only when the decision log records that the email was sent, and stopped with the reason when it was not (for example not sent: email:no_google_connector).
      • The approval of a remediation order already marked sent: its record keeps only the latest state.

      Approve an action

      Expand the action

      Tap it to read its artifacts: the email draft (TO:), the calendar invite and the ERP change.

      Select Approve

      The action moves to its next stage at once: its Approval step becomes Approved and it moves under the decided actions. The decision is queued on your device and sent to the server as soon as there is a connection, so an approval made offline is not lost.

      Read the outcome

      If the action has artifacts, the cockpit opens its decision artifact in the approved state. Otherwise it shows Action approved — queued for execution.

      An action that needs two approvers shows 1 of 2 approvals recorded after the first. When the server records an approval but a part of it could not run, a notice titled Recorded, but not executed lists each part with its reason.

      FACE has no way to edit a draft before it is sent. To change one, reject the action with your reason.

      Reject an action

      Every rejection asks for a reason first, because the reason is the only record of why the action was refused.

      Select Reject or the ✕ button

      The dialog Why are you rejecting this? opens and names the action.

      Write your reason

      Type it in Your reason. An empty reason is refused, and so is a reason too long to keep word for word.

      Select Reject with this reason

      The action’s later stages are marked stopped, and the rejection is kept in its decision record. A later decision is added to that record; it doesn’t replace the old one. Cancel leaves the action where it was.

      Remediation orders

      A remediation order is a REGULATORY ASSURANCE action. FACE sends no order anywhere: approving records a decision, and then a person sends the order and marks it sent. On an order:

      • NOTE is your reason in your own words. A rejection needs one.
      • CHANNEL is how you sent it (email, ticket, phone, and so on).
      • Assign opens Address this remediation, with DEPARTMENT, SERVICE PROVIDER and CONTACT.
      • Approve and Reject record a decision.
      • Mark sent — records that you sent it; FACE sends nothing records that you sent it.

      Analyze a document

      The upload button (Analyze document) sends a csv, txt, tsv or json file of up to 3 MB to the twins. Each step’s progress shows in a dialog, and the list is read again when you close it.

      When the list is empty

      The page has one empty state, and it says which of three things is true:

      You seeMeaning
      The twins are still working on this runThe fetch is still running; its current step is shown.
      This run failed before the twins proposed anythingThe fetch failed; its reason is shown.
      The twins finished and proposed nothingThe run’s data led to no action that needs a person.
      Digital twin reasoning…The actions are loading.
      Failed to fetch action queue: {error}The cockpit could not read the actions. This is not an empty run.

      Predictive maintenance and route proposals are on the map, on each vehicle’s and route’s card; the list links to them.

      RPCs behind these pages

      • AnalysisService.AnalyzeCausal, InferBayesian, for each action’s Why.
      • TwinsService.GetActionQueue (the run’s pending and approved actions), ExecuteAction (sent from the offline queue), ListDecisionLog (what an approval sent), UploadAnalyze (server stream), GetSavingsSummary, ListAssetLocations.
      • MapFeatureService.ListMapLayers, StreamMapFeatures (server stream).
      • TwinGraphService.GetTwinSiteDetails (a warehouse’s panel).
      • PostureService.GetHealthScore.
      • ComplianceService.AnalyzeCompliance, AssignRemediation, DecideRemediation.
      • runink.ui.siteplan.v1.SitePlanService, for the site plans in the twin details.

      See the API reference.
