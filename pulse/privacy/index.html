PULSE — Privacy Policy

This policy covers the PULSE app published by Runink (Android application id org.runink.pulse, and the web app) and the PULSE backend it talks to. For the rest of the Runink platform, see the general privacy policy.

PULSE is a marketing workspace sold to organisations per seat. You use it through your organisation's PULSE deployment.

Who can use PULSE

You sign in with your Google account. The first sign-in creates your PULSE account on your organisation's deployment, if your organisation has admitted your address. The app does not sell seats.

What PULSE collects

  • Your account: your name and email address from Google sign-in, and your role. Stored encrypted.
  • What you make in PULSE: audits of the websites you ask about, drafts, campaigns, plans, leads and follow-ups, your business profile and brand colours.
  • Images and files you add: logos, pictures and documents you pick or take with the camera app.
  • Voice commands: when you use the voice bar or the assistant call, your speech is sent to Runink's own speech-to-text service and turned into text. The audio is not kept. The server log records the size and loudness of the clip, not the words. The text becomes an assistant message, kept in your chat history like a typed one.
  • Digital twins: the photos, short videos and voice recordings you add to make your own presenter, the consent you record with each, and the presenter videos PULSE makes from them. See Digital twins below.
  • Connected accounts: when you connect LinkedIn, Instagram, YouTube, Google Business Profile, Search Console or an email mailbox, the access tokens they issue, stored encrypted, and what PULSE reads with them (your posts' results, reviews, comments and messages on those accounts).
  • Usage: how many tasks you ran today, for your seat's daily limit.

PULSE does not collect your location, contacts, calendar, health data or device identifiers. The app has no advertising or analytics SDK. Its only permissions on Android are the internet and the microphone.

Digital twins

A digital twin is a presenter for your own presentations and course materials. There are two kinds.

  • Built-in presenters are drawn, animated characters with a stock voice. They are not real people and hold no recording of anyone. Tapping one plays a short greeting.
  • Your own twin is made from your own photo or a short video of your face (a video of at most 60 seconds), and, if the voice service supports it, your own voice from a recording of 5 to 60 seconds that the app guides you through.

Consent. PULSE refuses a photo, video or recording that does not come with a consent you recorded in the last 24 hours: the consent text you were shown, a statement you give, and the time. The text says the voice, face and likeness are your own, or that the person in them agreed in writing, and that PULSE may use them only to make a digital version of you for your own presentations and course materials. PULSE does not check your statement against the recording.

Where it is processed. Your photo, video and voice recording are stored encrypted on Runink's servers and are not published or listed for anyone else. The voice sample is sent to Runink's own speech service to make your voice, and, for a lip-synced video, your photo and the narration go to Runink's own services on Runink's own hardware. No third-party AI service receives your face, voice or videos. PULSE does not identify people from their faces and does not match a face against any other.

Presenter videos. You can turn a script into a presenter video: a voice-over video, an animated presenter (an illustration), or a lip-synced video of your own twin. A lip-synced video is AI-generated. It shows an "AI-generated" label on the picture, and PULSE offers no way to switch the label off. Videos are yours: PULSE does not publish them unless you approve it, and publishing stays off until your organisation turns it on.

Withdrawing consent and deleting. You can withdraw consent at any time in the twin's screen. PULSE then deletes the recordings and the videos made from them. A withdrawn consent is not restored; to use your face or voice again you add the recording again with a new consent. Deleting a twin, replacing or removing its photo or video, and deleting your account delete the same things. A record that consent was given and withdrawn is kept until you delete your account. You can also edit a twin after you made it.

How long. PULSE keeps your recordings and videos until you withdraw consent or delete the twin or your account. It sets no shorter period.

AI phone calls to leads

PULSE can place a phone call in which an AI assistant talks to one of your leads for you. It is off by default. It needs your organisation's administrator to turn it on, the account's own setting, and, for each lead, a record that the lead agreed to calls from an AI. A call that comes from an outreach sequence is placed only after you approve that call's plan. PULSE refuses a call outside the allowed calling hours, to a number on your do-not-call list, or more often than the limits allow.

  • It says what it is. Every call opens by saying it is an automated AI assistant and whom it is calling for, then gives a recording notice. If the lead says to stop, the call ends and the number goes on your do-not-call list.
  • What is kept. PULSE keeps no audio. It keeps a written transcript, encrypted, with the call, readable by your organisation's editors and administrators, and the call's outcome and summary. The transcript is erased after 7 days unless your administrator set a longer period, 90 days at most. The outcome and summary stay on the lead.
  • The carrier. The call goes over Twilio, which carries the call and holds the number it calls. Runink's own voice speaks and Runink's own services understand: Twilio is not used for speech recognition or voice for PULSE.

Questions from the assistant to you

When the assistant cannot answer from your own data, it may ask the account's owner one short question. The question is always shown in the app. It is also sent by text message or WhatsApp to the owner's own contact only if the owner opted in (off by default), approved the question, and it is outside the owner's quiet hours. The owner can reply STOP to withdraw.

Email

PULSE sends email only from your own connected mailbox (Gmail, Microsoft 365 or an SMTP server), never from a shared sender, and only when your organisation has turned sending on. Each email carries your identity, postal address and an unsubscribe link. PULSE reads replies in that mailbox to stop a sequence and to show you the answer. Disconnecting the mailbox removes its tokens.

Website chat and Google Business Profile

If you embed PULSE's chat on your own website, a visitor's messages are kept for 7 days unless your organisation sets another period (30 days at most), and then erased. The chat says it is an AI assistant, and stores no IP address. Contact details are kept only if the visitor submits the form. If you connect Google Business Profile, PULSE reads your locations, reviews and results with your own Google sign-in and posts or replies only when you approve.

Data about other people

PULSE finds business leads from public sources and from your website form: company names, public business contact details and the pages they came from. It keeps them for your organisation's use in PULSE. PULSE is for business-to-business contact: it uses business addresses, keeps a suppression list that every outreach sequence respects, and a do-not-call list for calls. Someone who is a lead can ask your organisation, or us at contact@runink.org, to remove their details. When a lead takes part in an AI call, the call's transcript is handled as described above.

How it is used and shared

  • Your data is used to run PULSE for you and your organisation.
  • AI in PULSE runs on Runink's own servers. No third-party AI service receives your data.
  • PULSE publishes to LinkedIn, Instagram or YouTube, sends an email from your mailbox, or sends a WhatsApp or SMS message only after you approve it, and only when your organisation has turned publishing on.
  • Service providers act for Runink: Twilio carries calls and messages, and your own Google, Microsoft, LinkedIn, Meta and YouTube accounts receive only what you send them.
  • PULSE can report each agent run to Runink TIDDE's console for audit. Nothing is sent today: this stays off until it is approved and this page says so.
  • Runink does not sell your data.

Deleting your account

In the app: Account › Privacy and your account › Delete my account. This signs you out everywhere and deletes your account and what PULSE keeps under it: your twins, recordings and presenter videos, call transcripts, leads, connections and settings. Earlier encrypted versions stay in storage history for about a day, until it is compacted and cleaned. Posts already published stay on the networks they went to.

Without the app: email contact@runink.org from your account's address, with the subject "Delete my PULSE account". What is deleted and what is kept: Delete your account.

Children

PULSE is a marketing workspace for businesses. It is not directed at children and is for people aged 18 and over; only people an organisation has admitted can use it.

Changes

We will update this page when PULSE's behaviour changes, and update the date below.

Your rights and complaints

Runink is published by Technologies Runink Inc., Montréal, Québec, Canada.

You can ask us to show you the personal information we hold about you, to correct it, to delete it, or to give you a copy. Email contact@runink.org. We read that mailbox, and we answer within 30 days.

If you are in the European Union or the United Kingdom, you also have the right to object to or restrict how we use your data, and to complain to your own data protection authority. If you are in Canada, the rights above come from Québec's Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector and from federal law. For your face and voice recordings, withdrawing consent in the twin's screen is the fastest route; you can also write to us.

If you are not satisfied with our answer, you can complain to the Commission d'accès à l'information du Québec or to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada.

This policy is governed by the laws of Québec and the federal laws of Canada that apply there.
