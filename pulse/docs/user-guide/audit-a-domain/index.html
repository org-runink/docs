Audit a domain

Audit a domain

Runink PULSE: Prescriptive Unified Lead & Social Engine can fill most of its pages from one sentence. Type Audit and a web address into the assistant, and PULSE reads the site, scores it, writes the diagnosis, and copies the result into the pages that act on it. You do not have to know what else to ask for.

Each stage on the way is tracked. A stage that wrote something says where its result came from. A stage that could not finish says why, and the pages it would have filled stay empty rather than showing something made up.

What you type

Open the assistant and send one line with a verb and a domain:

Audit example.com

The verbs PULSE recognizes are audit, analyse (or analyze), scan, review, inspect, and score. A full address such as https://example.com/pricing works too; PULSE keeps only the host name, lower-cased.

  • A line that contains a question mark is treated as a question and goes to the model as an ordinary chat turn. To run the audit, leave the question mark out.
  • An IP address or localhost is refused with "…" is not a domain (expected something like example.com).
  • Running an audit needs the editor or admin role. A viewer can read the result but not start one. See What each role may call.

The reply is a card titled Audit of example.com, with one row per stage.

Attach your own material

The new-audit form also takes photos or a short video of your store or product, product documents and voice notes, so the audit compares your site against them: what the material shows that the site does not say, where the two disagree, and which benefits the site never communicates.

Kind Accepted Limits What PULSE reads
Photo JPEG, PNG, WebP (not HEIC: share it as JPEG) 15 MB A description and the visible text
Video MP4, MOV, WebM 60 MB, 60 seconds Frames from the video, described like photos
Document PDF, DOCX (50 pages, 20 MB); TXT, MD (2 MB) The text. A scanned PDF has none; text recognition is not run
Voice note WAV, WebM, OGG, M4A, MP3 20 MB, 5 minutes A transcript

Each file is sealed in PULSE's encrypted store as it arrives; it is never kept as a plain file. Only you can see your attachments. One that no audit used is removed after 24 hours; one an audit used is removed 30 days after that audit started. You can delete one at any time.

When something cannot be read, the attachment says so, and the audit does not use it: image understanding unavailable (this deployment cannot read images), frames not analysed, transcription unavailable, or no text found. PULSE never describes a photo it could not look at.

The Your attached material stage on the card lists what the diagnosis was given. Findings that come from the material name the attachment they come from, and are reviewed like every other part of the plan.

If an audit of the same domain is already running, a new audit with attachments the running one was not started with is refused with an audit of example.com is already running, rather than joining it without them. Your attachments are kept: run the audit again when the current one finishes.

How long it takes

Minutes. When another audit is running on the same deployment, your stages wait their turn, and the card shows Waiting for the model (position N in the queue).

You can leave the page. The audit keeps running on the server, and each page fills in as its stage finishes. When you come back to the assistant, the card reconnects to the run.

While it runs, the title line of every page follows it: the domain, whether it is still running and how it ended, and the reason the run gives. Tapping it opens the check-up on Brand Analysis.

The stages

The card lists every stage of the audit as it runs, under the heading Running — each page fills in as its stage finishes.

Every figure in the stages from Drafts for approval to Market summary comes from the diagnosis; none is invented along the way.

Guest opportunities whose address the search actually returned are kept with their source and a Relevance (search fit) score, never a follower figure. They show under Brand Analysis › Partners to contact. See The Brand Analysis pages.

Content ideas for your customers (deep research)

At the end of a check-up PULSE turns what it measured into content ideas, shown on Brand Analysis. Each idea is a scenario: one kind of customer, one problem they have, one angle (teach, show proof, behind the scenes, offer, event), and the Instagram or YouTube formats it suits, with post ideas and the findings that back it.

  • What it sells first. The offerings stage lists what your site says you sell, each with the site's own words. The plan gate reviews them, and the check-up line says how many were kept and dropped. Ideas and content-gap cards are tied to one of these.
  • What people search for. With Google Search Console connected, the searches people type before they find your site, and where your site ranks for them, are the first measured demand an idea can cite.
  • Who it is for comes from what you told PULSE, the leads the audit found, or the diagnosis's audience. PULSE never invents a customer. See Personas.
  • Each scenario is checked by the guardrails and the plan gate. Ideas that do not pass are listed as held back, with the reason.
  • Your Instagram week: a 7-day plan across several scenarios.

Tune (on the card, or Fine-tune the content ideas before a check-up) sets who you sell to, what you sell, where you post, how often, topics to focus on or avoid, language, region and depth. Quick is the default; Deep asks PULSE's model to think each idea through first and takes longer. Saving on an existing check-up makes new ideas from the same findings.

Start over

Settings › Account and the check-up history on Brand Analysis have Start over: type your domain to delete every check-up of this account (or agency client) and what it made: findings, content ideas, campaign suggestions you did not accept, drafts you did not approve. You can also tick accepted campaigns, the leads check-ups found, and approved or scheduled posts not sent yet. Anything published, its measured results and your connected accounts are never deleted. It is refused while a check-up is running.

What each status means

Status Meaning
done The stage wrote at least one row, or a source that really answered found nothing. The row shows where the result came from: sourced from the web, inferred by the model, or sourced and inferred, plus the first source address.
failed The stage ran and could not write anything, or had nothing to work from. The row gives the reason, for example had 4 to write, wrote 0 or no trend search answered (0 of 3 queries). The page it feeds stays empty.
skipped The stage did not run. Either it was blocked because a stage it depends on did not finish (the reason starts with blocked:), or it is the publishing schedule and every channel it would queue on is switched off.
waiting The stage is in the queue for the model, at the position shown.

A finding of "0" counts as done only when the source answered. When no search answered at all, the stage is failed: silence is not reported as an empty result.

Why "Publishing schedule" says skipped

The schedule stage puts each planned date on a draft of the same channel. It queues nothing: a post waits for your approval, and approving a draft while its date is still ahead schedules it for that date (on a channel that is switched on). When the plan has no draft to put a date on, the stage ends skipped with no draft to put the plan's times on (the tasks are on the 90-day plan). That is not a fault and does not make the audit partial.

A post whose date passes while it is still waiting for approval is marked missed with the reason, and is never sent late: reschedule it from the Scheduled column on Campaigns.

How the audit ends

Card heading Meaning
Done — every page was populated Every stage is done. The publishing schedule may be skipped.
Partly done — some pages are still empty (see the failed stages) Some stages finished and some did not. The failed and blocked stages are listed with their reasons.
The audit failed No stage that fills a page finished.

Resume, run again, or reuse

  • Resume the audit runs it again from the first stage that is not done. Stages already done on the same input are not repeated, and nothing they wrote is written twice.
  • Run it again starts a fresh audit even if one finished recently.
  • Asking again within 24 hours of a finished audit returns that audit as it is, with a note saying when it ran. Use Run it again to force a new one.
  • Asking while an audit of the same domain is already running joins it. The card says Joined the audit already running for this domain and replays every stage from the start.

A fresh run never switches the campaign to active: it updates the one campaign it keeps for this domain and leaves its status alone. It never deletes or changes drafts that earlier runs left in Drafts to approve.

What is never inferred

The audit writes no performance numbers.

  • Results are not filled by an audit. Impressions, clicks, conversions, and return come only from the accounts you connect.
  • Partners and shows found by an audit carry no follower, audience, or engagement figure. Their score measures how well the show fits your niche, and the label says so.
  • Audience sizes on the market pages are shown only when the search results stated them. An unknown size stays unknown; it is never estimated.
  • The site score comes from deterministic checks on the live site. Checks that need a data source you have not connected are left out and listed, not counted as zero.

Where to see past audits

Every audit, with its stages and their reasons, is kept per user. AuditPipelineService/ListAuditRuns lists yours, newest first, and GetAuditRun returns one. See the API reference.

On Brand Analysis, Your check-ups lists them with their date, how many parts finished and their status. Tap one and the whole page shows it; the latest is shown by default. Delete one, or Clear all previous check-ups (type the domain to confirm; the latest stays unless you tick it). Deleting removes the check-up's steps, what it found and its AI-visibility results; your leads, campaigns, drafts and site-score history stay.
