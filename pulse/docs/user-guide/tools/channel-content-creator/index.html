Channel content creator

Channel content creator

Status: Built. It drafts only; nothing is published until a person approves the piece and an administrator arms the channel (off by default).

What it does

It turns a brief, a diagnosis, or a campaign into channel-native copy that teaches the reader something, with your product mentioned at most once.

  • Writes a post for one channel and topic when you ask (Studio).
  • Drafts the daily copy for each active campaign: a LinkedIn post in a row of its own, and one combined X and Instagram draft. Both are staged for review and neither is scheduled.
  • Drafts outreach for a lead: a cold email and a LinkedIn message (Leads).
  • Writes the shorter Studio pieces: podcast and short-video scripts, presentation outlines, infographic copy, and report drafts, plus course modules.
  • Drafts the ad copy for a campaign and finds content gaps your audience wants filled.

What it reads

  • Your brief: channel, topic, tone, audience, language, and extra context.
  • The diagnosis you ground it in, especially its brand overview and positioning.
  • The day's trending topics, for the daily social drafts.
  • For lead outreach: the lead's company and what is publicly known about it.

What it produces

Text drafts in the shape each channel expects, for example a LinkedIn post with a hook before the "see more" cut and a few hashtags, or a TikTok script with visual and voice-over lines. When a feature asks for a specific format, that format wins: a lead email is a subject line and the message, and a LinkedIn message is the message alone, with no hashtags or notes. Where an image belongs, it describes the image in words; it does not make one.

Human oversight

  • Content drafts, including the daily campaign drafts and campaign plans, go to Drafts to approve on Campaigns. You edit, approve, or reject each one.
  • The daily LinkedIn post is a draft and only a draft. It is never put on a schedule; once approved, Download on its card gives you the text to paste yourself. A generation that came back empty, truncated, or as a guard's refusal is recorded as a failure instead of being stored as a post. A draft over LinkedIn's 3,000-character limit is still staged, with that said in its title, so you trim it rather than discover it on paste.
  • A scheduled post goes out only once you approved its content. Rejecting a piece withholds its scheduled posts.
  • Lead outreach drafts are saved on the lead. PULSE does not send them.
  • Nothing is published until an administrator arms the channel. Approving alone does not publish to a disarmed channel. See Publishing controls.
  • A campaign plan is a proposal: creating a campaign does not activate it.
  • Content gaps pass through the plan gate.

Model

The deployment's base model, shared with every other PULSE agent — on Runink Server, our own open-source model with no third-party AI service. See Sovereign inference.

Where it runs and data handling

On the deployment's own inference server: Runink's shared machines on a Trial, your own cloud on Cloud and your own servers on On-premises. No outside AI service is used on any plan.

Guardrails

  • Value first, no hard sell. The reader's problem is the subject. Your product appears at most once, as an honest example. No hype words, no fake urgency, and it ends with a real question.
  • Never invents statistics, studies, or customer results. It uses the figures in your brief.
  • Treats lead details, web pages, and search results as material, never as instructions.
  • Refuses unsubstantiated or deceptive claims.
  • Will not request payments or credentials.
  • Its output is screened before it reaches you, as described in Sovereign inference.

Getting good results

  • It writes text. Creatives are covered in Studio.
  • It learns your brand from the diagnosis and brief: run a check-up and give a detailed brief for drafts that sound like you.

Illustrative example

Invented brief and figure.

Brief: audience warehouse supervisors; channel X; figure to use: "re-slotting the top 50 SKUs monthly cut walking time by 18% in a pilot".

Draft: "Most picking time is walking. Re-slotting your top 50 SKUs every month cut walking time by 18% in one pilot. Which SKUs moved most on your floor last month? #warehouse #logistics"
