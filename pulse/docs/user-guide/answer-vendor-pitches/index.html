Answer vendor pitches

Answer vendor pitches

Companies and freelancers write to you to sell what you sell, or something close to it: an agency, an outsourcing team, a lead-generation or marketing tool. Answer vendor pitches in Runink PULSE: Prescriptive Unified Lead & Social Engine answers them for you with your own counter-offer: become our client, or partner with us, with your team paid upfront as your terms say. A vendor who answers with interest becomes a record in your partner pipeline.

It is off until you switch it on, in Settings › Account › Answer vendor pitches, for your account only. Switching it on means that, from then on, a pitch PULSE is sure about is answered without asking you first. A message it is not sure about waits for you.

Your terms are the only wording

You fill in, in your own words:

  • what you offer a vendor who becomes your client;
  • what you offer a vendor who becomes your partner;
  • how your team is paid upfront;
  • your signature, and optionally a booking link (https).

PULSE writes the reply from these and from the vendor's own words, quoted back. It never adds a price, a term, a discount or a figure of its own: a number appears only if you typed it. Until every term and your Outreach sender name and postal address are filled in, PULSE does nothing, and the card lists exactly what is missing. There are no default terms.

The reply is in the vendor's language when it is English, French, Spanish or Portuguese, otherwise English. It says it was written by an AI assistant and sent on behalf of your team, and carries your sender name, postal address and a one-click unsubscribe link, like your other emails.

What it answers, and what it never does

PULSE decides what a message is from fixed rules, not from guesses. It answers only an unsolicited pitch for a service or tool. It never answers:

  • a lead, a customer, someone already in your pipeline, or a reply in a conversation you already have;
  • your own address or domain;
  • an automatic message: an out-of-office, a bounce, a mailing list, a no-reply sender;
  • spam or a phishing attempt (no link is opened);
  • anything that tries to give PULSE instructions.

It answers each sender once, and at most ten a day. Someone who writes "stop", "unsubscribe" or the same in French, Spanish or Portuguese goes on your suppression list and nothing more is sent to them; "not interested" ends their record.

Where it listens

Each channel is shown on the card with what is on, what is off and why, taken from what is really set up:

  • Your website form: the vendor's email is in the enquiry, and the answer goes from your own mailbox. A pitch PULSE answers is not made into a lead.
  • Instagram direct messages: through the same reader as Autopilot, inside Instagram's 24-hour answer window.
  • E-mail: PULSE follows replies to the emails it sent. To also see a pitch that arrives only in your mailbox, switch on Read my inbox for vendor pitches (below). It is off until you do, Gmail only for now, and the card says why when it cannot read (reconnect to allow, Gmail only, slowed down by the provider).
  • LinkedIn: not available. LinkedIn gives PULSE no way to read direct messages.
  • WhatsApp and SMS: not available. Only contacts you have allowed reach PULSE, and each message needs a person's confirm tap.
  • Website chat: not available. A visitor stays anonymous, so there is no address to answer.

Read my inbox for vendor pitches

A second switch in Settings › Account, separate from the one above and OFF until you turn it on. Both must be on. With it on, PULSE reads new mail in your connected Gmail inbox, read-only, and gives each message to the same answering steps as a website enquiry.

  • What it reads: the inbox folder only. A sub-option, also off, adds the promotions, updates, social and forums tabs. Spam is never read, and mail you sent is never looked at.
  • From when: only mail received after you switched it on. The moment is shown on the card. Turning it off and on again, or adding the promotions option, moves that moment forward. The first read goes back no further than 7 days from it.
  • What it never answers: automatic replies, mailing lists, bulk mail and no-reply senders (the usual headers say so), a message that is itself a reply in a thread, and anyone you already know.
  • What it keeps: a position marker and counts, nothing of the messages. The sender, subject and a short snippet are read for the moment and never written to a log; what a pitch record keeps is what it kept before.
  • What the card shows: the last read time, counts by kind (read, skipped because sent, spam or older than the switch, and each class of pitch) and, when something is wrong, the reason in words: reconnect your mailbox and allow reading, Gmail only for now, the provider asked PULSE to slow down (with the time of the next try), the provider could not be reached, or the daily limit was reached.

Safety rules that still apply

An answer goes out only if your administrator has armed sending, the channel's own switch is on, and My rules and the conscience check allow it: your never-contact list, working hours, daily caps and any rule that asks for approval of a first contact. Each answer is recorded, and What PULSE did and why shows what was checked. If a rule holds a reply, it waits for you.

What you do

Under Leads › Partners, Vendors who answered our offer lists every vendor with its stage: pitched, answered, interested, call proposed, agreed or declined. PULSE moves a record forward as the vendor answers, and proposes; only you mark a vendor agreed.

A reply that waits for you shows its text with Approve (sent through the same checks), Edit and Reject. The card keeps a short log of what PULSE did: the channel, the kind of message and the outcome, never the vendor's words.

From a pitch to a lead or a partner

A vendor is not only someone to answer. When a sender shows interest in your counter-offer, or the message itself shows they could buy from you, PULSE proposes two things and you decide:

  • Partner: the record is already in your partner pipeline.
  • Lead: Could this vendor be a lead? with Accept and Dismiss. Accepting makes a lead from a business email address (never a personal mailbox, never a sender who asked to stop), with the source vendor pitch and the stage new. If you already have a lead at that address, the two are linked, not duplicated. Nothing is sent and no outreach sequence starts: your outreach approval rules are unchanged.

A sender stays one record, whether it moves between partner and lead.

What vendors pitching us tell us

From each pitch PULSE keeps a few codes, not the message: what the sender sells (a category such as SEO, lead generation, outsourcing or software), the differentiators they claim (a free audit, a guarantee, and so on), the kind of pricing they name (per seat, per lead, retainer, performance, flat, or not stated; never the amounts), the channel, which of your own offerings the message overlaps and what the sender did after your counter-offer.

Settings › Account › What vendors pitching us tell us adds them up over a window: the most common categories, which are rising, the common claims, how many overlap what you sell, and categories senders pitch that your offerings never mention. Every line is what senders claimed, never a fact about the market, and each shows how many pitches are behind it, with the records in Leads › Partners. A count nobody measured is not shown, never shown as zero. Until the window and the minimum number of pitches are set by your administrator, and until that many pitches have arrived, the card says so and shows nothing.

Each week with something measured, PULSE adds a weekly digest to the card for you to read and mark reviewed. It is shown in the app only; nothing is emailed or sent. You can also ask the assistant about these insights.

What PULSE keeps, and for how long

For each pitch it answers, PULSE keeps a summary: the sender's address and company, one sentence they wrote about what they sell, the reply it drafted, and the codes above. It does not keep the message itself. After 90 days (your administrator can change this) the address, company, quoted sentence and draft are erased; the codes, the stage and a one-way marker stay, so the same sender is still answered only once and the insights still count. A reply still waiting for you, or a lead proposal you have not decided, is kept until you decide. Deleting your account removes all of it, and your data export includes it.
