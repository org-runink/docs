Leads

Leads

Leads (/leads) is everyone outside your business in one page: everyone who could buy from you (the people PULSE found and the public tenders that need what you sell) on one board, and, on the Partners canvas beside them, the partners, creators and shows you work with. A switch at the top moves between the two.

From top to bottom, the Leads canvas has: the HubSpot import (when HubSpot is connected), Who you're looking for, To do today, the counts, the replies to your emails, the filter row, and the board.

The board

One board, five lanes: New, Talking, Proposal, Won, Lost. People and public tenders share them:

Lane A person A public tender
New found, not contacted yet found by PULSE
Talking contacted interested, or preparing a response
Proposal qualified: the offer is being made response submitted
Won / Lost the deal's outcome the buyer's answer

Move on a card sets its lane. A move that means nothing for that kind of card is refused, with the reason, and nothing changes: a tender is won or lost only after you submitted a response (move it to Proposal first); a tender you are not answering goes away with Not for us.

Each person is a card; tap it to open it. An open card shows the lead's details, its next step, its History (the emails and AI calls to it) and its action row:

  • Draft writes a message for this lead: a Cold email, a Cold-call script or a LinkedIn DM. PULSE never sends a draft by itself.
  • Send (on an email draft) sends it from your own mailbox. See Email.
  • Call places an AI call, where your administrator has allowed them.
  • Move changes the lane.
  • Add deal value / Edit deal value records what the deal is worth.
  • Automate opens the lead's own automation; see Automations.

A tender's card is described under Public tenders.

Filters

The filter row shows All, People or Public tenders. While tenders are shown, it also narrows them by country and by type of work (what you sell that the tender matched), and opens Tender settings.

Where leads come from

Source How
Your check-ups A check-up finds leads for your niche and puts them in New, with the contact their page publishes and where they were found.
Find → People who could buy from you Finds businesses that publish a way to reach them and writes a first email, a call script and a LinkedIn message for each. It asks for a niche, a region, who buys from you, and competitors to leave out, filled in from your last check-up. 10 leads a run by default, at most 25.
Find → Public tenders: look now Reads every tender source now. See Public tenders.
Add lead By hand. A lead needs at least a name, an email or a company. On a phone with no signal, it is saved and marked Queued until you are back online.
Your website's contact form Each enquiry becomes a lead in New. See Website form.
HubSpot Sync from HubSpot imports your contacts. The bar says when it last synced and how many it read, or why it failed.

PULSE shows HubSpot's own lifecycle stage on a lead as it is, and does not score HubSpot leads itself. The board lane is PULSE's own.

To do today

To do today lists, for each lead that is due, one next step in plain words, such as "send a follow-up email" or "place an AI call to book a meeting", with Do it and Skip (moves it to tomorrow). A lead with no email or phone says so. It also lists:

  • the public tenders you are working on (not yet submitted) that close within 14 days, soonest first;
  • leads who replied to an email you sent, with the first words of the reply and Answer;
  • leads PULSE's daily check says may go quiet, with its reason;
  • your Weekly check-in: new followers, posts, engagement, leads and how satisfied you are this week, plus what worked and what didn't. Your check-ins are what Your plan measures progress with, and Recalculate my plan from these check-ins re-plans the rest of the cycle;
  • what Autopilot needs from you, for example an account it stopped posting to and why.

Deal value and won revenue

Moving a lead to Won asks what the deal was worth: an amount and a three-letter currency code (such as EUR). A lead cannot be marked won without a value. You can also give any open lead a value.

The counts above the board show Won this month (per currency, never added across currencies), then, for New, Talking and Proposal, the open value of the people in that lane and how many tenders are in it, and Tenders closing within 14 days. Leads with no value are counted as "not valued", never as 0; before PULSE first looked for tenders their counts say tenders not counted yet, never 0. Deal values stay in PULSE; HubSpot receives contacts only.

Email from your own mailbox

Connect your mailbox under Settings → Integrations → Email (Gmail, Microsoft 365 or SMTP). Then Send on an email draft sends the newest draft to the lead from your address, records it in the lead's history, and moves a lead in New to Talking. Send asks you to confirm first. It is not offered, and the sheet says why, when:

  • you are a viewer (sending needs an editor or admin);
  • your administrator switched email sending off;
  • no mailbox is connected, or it needs attention;
  • you reached the daily limit.

With Gmail or Microsoft 365, PULSE notices who replied and lists them under To do today. With SMTP it cannot read replies, and the page says so.

Public tenders

Public tenders and requests for proposals that need what you sell (a council website, an app, videos for a campaign) are cards on the Leads board. PULSE reads official open tender data every day and matches it on what your check-up found you sell. You decide, and you submit: PULSE reads and drafts, and never submits anything.

Where PULSE looks

Source Needs
EU tenders (TED) Nothing
UK Find a Tender · UK Contracts Finder Nothing
Brazil public purchases (PNCP) Nothing
CanadaBuys Nothing
Requests for proposals on the web Nothing
US federal (SAM.gov) Your own SAM.gov public API key, pasted on the SAM.gov card in Settings → Integrations

With Public tenders shown, the filter row says when PULSE last looked, and Where PULSE looked says, for each source, what the last look got: notices read, nothing new, could not be read (with the reason), or not connected (with what to do). These are four different answers, never an empty list.

PULSE looks once a day. Find → Public tenders: look now looks straight away. If your administrator switched the daily look off, the page says so and looking now still works.

A tender's card

Each tender's card shows a Public tender chip (Request for proposals for a private buyer), the title, the buyer and country, the closing date with how many days are left (No deadline stated when the notice gives none), the source, and what it matched. While you work on it, it also says Interested or Preparing. Open, it shows the value (Value not stated when the notice gives none), the words and codes it matched on, Open the notice, and its actions:

  • Prepare response writes a response draft (Draft again to redo it).
  • Move moves the card between lanes; in Talking it also marks it as interested or as preparing a response.
  • Not for us sets it aside, with your reason.
  • Automate opens its own automation.

The response draft

Draft response (not sent) lays out: Why you, What the notice asks, what is Answered, what is Not covered: add it yourself, Documents to prepare, Before you send, the deadline and how to submit. Copy draft copies it as plain text. Check it, complete it, send it yourself, then move the card to Proposal. A draft PULSE's checks or reviewer refused says Not drafted and why.

Tender settings

Tender settings sets the countries PULSE looks in (two-letter codes; empty means all), notices too close to their deadline to bother with, reminders before a deadline, the value range and its currency, public and private buyers, whether new matches show in the bell, and whether PULSE drafts a response for the best new matches by itself (this uses model time).

Partners

The Partners canvas lists the partners your latest check-up found, with how to reach them and where they were found, then the partners, creators and shows you follow. Add a partner adds one by hand. Each partner has Partnership message, which drafts messages for you to copy and send yourself (PULSE does not send these), and its own automation. Stop following removes a partner; a later check-up that finds them again does not add them back.

Freelance & contracts

Freelance & contracts is a category of the Leads board, beside People and Public tenders: everything that can bring money is in one place. Under All its items sit in New with the rest, each card badged with its category; in each lane the cards that state money come first. Save as lead on an item makes it your own lead in New, valued from its published total (a tender's estimated value) when it states one, else "not valued"; it then moves New → Talking (Contacted) → Proposal (Qualified) → Won or Lost like any lead and counts in the pipeline strip. An item you have not saved is read when you open Leads and never stored.

The category lists contracts that match your expertise: the skills buyers post contracts for, such as "data engineering", "AI governance" or "Kubernetes". PULSE takes them from the check-up you opened in this session:

  • what your site says you sell, and the skills its own words name;
  • what you named in Tune;
  • when those give little or the check-up could not read what you sell, the skills your site's own product, service and pricing pages name.

The check-up's niche is never a term on its own; it only adds weight to a term that shares a word with it. Each term shows where it came from and its weight. Edit in Tune opens Tune, where Your expertise replaces the inferred terms with yours (leave it empty to let PULSE infer them).

Under the category, a header shows those terms (Edit in Tune) and a filter chooses the segment:

  • Public: calls for tenders from public buyers, read from TED (EU, IT, data and R&D codes together with your terms), Contracts Finder (UK) and PNCP (Brazil).
  • Private: companies hiring contractors. PULSE reads the public job boards of the companies the check-up found (its leads, partners and competitors) on Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby, then the job boards Jobicy and Himalayas, which rank below. Each item says Direct or Via agency: via agency when the company's name says it is a staffing or recruiting firm, or the posting says it is written for a client.
  • All: both, with the same role from two sources listed once. The filter also offers Direct and Via agency on their own.

Each item shows its buyer or company, its segment, its contract type and duration, the value or rate as published (or "not published"), its closing date, its relevance score and the terms it matched, and a link to its source. The score adds each matched term's weight, twice when the term is in the title, plus 0.5 for a tender filed under an IT, data or R&D code. Items that match no term or score under 1 are not listed; the header says how many, and how many permanent roles and duplicates were left out. Nothing is stored: the list is read when you open Leads.

If PULSE knows no expertise yet, the header says why and asks you to name it in Tune or run the check-up again. If a segment has nothing, it lists the terms searched, each source checked with what it returned, and the next step. The sources PULSE checked and does not read (for example SAM.gov, which needs a key, and Hacker News, whose terms forbid automated reading) are listed with the reason.

Calls

Call on a lead opens the AI call. It is refused, with every reason listed, unless your administrator has turned AI calls on and the lead gave consent; see AI calls to leads. A lead can also be put on the do-not-call list from its AI call sheet.

Warning: Cold calling and cold email are regulated where you and your leads are: consent, calling hours, do-not-call lists, and unsubscribe rules. Check the rules that apply to you before you contact real people.
