What PULSE has learned about your market

What PULSE has learned about your market

PULSE can keep a short list of plain statements about your market, built only from counts of your own results: which channels and campaigns brought leads, which posts got readable results, how many replies came in. It is off until you switch on Learn from my results every week in Settings › Account. Nothing is learned from a result with too little evidence, and a figure nobody measured is never kept as zero.

Each entry says where it comes from (observed, stated or inferred), how confident it is, how many results support it and how many said something different. Entries hold no message text and no contact details: a statement with a link, an e-mail address or a phone number is refused before it is kept.

You decide what stays. An editor or administrator can forget an entry (it stays listed as forgotten, with the reason), correct it in their own words, or delete everything that is kept. What you corrected or forgot is never overwritten and never brought back by PULSE.

The tidy-up

When the app is quiet, PULSE tidies the list once a day for accounts that switched learning on. It sets aside entries that have not been confirmed for a long time and statements that were recorded twice, and it reports entries whose results disagree without touching them. It never touches an entry you corrected or forgot.

When a tidy-up found or set aside something, Settings shows one line with the date and the counts, for example "2 not confirmed for a long time, 1 repeated, 3 with evidence that disagrees". When it had nothing to report, nothing is shown. Entries set aside stay in the list as forgotten, and correcting one brings it back.

Export and import (Open Knowledge Format)

Your list is yours to take with you. In Settings › Account, Export what PULSE learned (OKF) saves what PULSE learned about your market, and only that, as a bundle in the open Open Knowledge Format (a ZIP of plain text files). Entries you forgot are left out; entries you corrected are marked as corrected by a person. The download link works for a short time, and you can export once an hour. An editor or administrator, signed in as a person, asks for it; the bundle is kept sealed under your account and removed after two hours.

Import knowledge (OKF) reads a bundle your own account exported back into the list, for example after you deleted everything. Learning must be switched on first. Three things to know:

  • Only your own. A bundle from another account, or from another Runink app, is refused, and so is a file that does not follow the format. The reason is said in plain words.
  • Not yet confirmed. Every imported entry is marked "Imported, not yet confirmed" and counts for little. Whatever the bundle says about itself, for example that a person confirmed it, is ignored. Correct an entry to confirm it.
  • Nothing you decided is changed. An entry you corrected or forgot, and one already in the list, is left as it is. Statements with a link, an e-mail address, a phone number or too much text are left out and counted, and at most 300 imported entries are kept per account.

The file is read in memory only and is limited in size and number of files, so a damaged or oversized file is refused before anything is read. An export or import writes one line with counts only in the server log, never a statement.

Drafting with what PULSE learned

A second switch, Use what PULSE learned when drafting (Settings › Account, right under the learning switch), lets PULSE use some of these entries when it writes a post, caption, story, reel or video script, a LinkedIn company update, a newsletter or a blog article. It is off until you switch it on, and it works only while weekly learning is on too.

What PULSE hands the writer:

  • A few entries only. At most five (your administrator can set up to ten), chosen by plain rules, not by a model: the ones that name the channel being written for or a word of your topic first, then the most confident, then the most recent. Two drafts with the same inputs get the same entries.
  • Only entries PULSE can stand behind. An entry you forgot, one set aside as not confirmed for a long time, one whose results disagree, one imported from a bundle and not yet confirmed, and one that has faded below the confidence PULSE needs are never used. Only entries built from counts of your own results (channels, campaigns, posts and replies) are used, never anything about a customer or a lead, and never text with a link, an e-mail address or a phone number.
  • As data, not instructions. The writer is told to use them only where they fit and never to state a figure they do not hold. A draft that cites one of these figures is checked against it like any other fact you gave PULSE; a draft that invents a figure is still held back.
  • Never in a reply or a message to a person. Comment replies, direct messages, review replies and every extraction or audit step never see what PULSE learned.

Under the switch Settings says what is missing: that learning is still off, that the server still needs a setting, or No learned facts yet, which means no entry qualifies yet and nothing is added to drafts (it is not a count of zero results).

Which facts did a draft use? A draft written with learned facts shows Informed by N learned facts in Studio. Tap it to see the entries, as they are in your list now: a fact you forgot since says so. The draft itself keeps only the entries' numbers, not a second copy of their text. If you forget or correct an entry, the next draft changes; drafts already written keep what they were written with. Each saved draft writes one line with counts only in the server log, never a statement.
