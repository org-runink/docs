Measured and not measured

Measured and not measured

PULSE shows a figure only when something measured it. A figure nobody measured is not drawn as 0 and is not estimated: it says Not measured, or shows —. A 0 is a real answer ("we looked and there were none"); "not measured" means nobody looked.

The answers you will see

What the app says What it means
A figure A source answered: your site, the web, a platform, a connected account, or PULSE's own records.
Counted From PULSE's own records (posts sent, drafts approved, tasks done). A 0 here is a real 0.
Nothing found / None in the last 28 days The source answered and had nothing.
Not measured / — No source answered. No figure is shown.
Needs a connection / Connect … Only an account you have not connected can answer it.
Could not be read The source exists but did not answer right now, usually with the reason. Try again later.
Not recorded It happened, but PULSE kept no record of it.
not yet It has not happened.

Three of these are easy to confuse, and the app keeps them apart on purpose: an empty answer, no answer, and an answer that could not be read. A page that could not read something says so rather than showing an empty list.

Where the figures come from

Source Examples
Your check-up Site score, AI and search visibility, Google Discover readiness, leads, competitors, partners
PULSE's own records What PULSE did: posts sent, drafts approved, outreach drafted, tasks done
The platforms Each post's results as Instagram, LinkedIn or YouTube report them
Accounts you connect Google Analytics, Google Ads, Google Search Console
You Your weekly reviews on the 90-day plan, your own figures for its targets, deal values

A figure a platform does not report for a kind of post is "not measured" for that post, never 0.

Lines over time

A line is drawn only through measured points, and starts once there are two. A day or a check-up that measured nothing is a gap, not a 0.

When the model estimates

Some numbers are the model's judgement, not a measurement. The app labels them as such, for example a trending topic's heat … (model estimate) or a content gap's demand and opportunity scores. A lead's fit tier (high, medium, low) is not a probability.

In a report, the figures come from your measured records. See Reports.
